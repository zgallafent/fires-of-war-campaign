Lamara is a rock gnome in [[Mazrunas]]. She was born in [[Leovolan]] and married [[Bolren]]. They moved to [[Wicksbend]] shortly after their son, [[Hazelmere]], was born. After the city was sacked, she moved to Mazrunas with plans to return to [[Zemvalda]] when the border reopened.
## Stories
### A Morning in Mazrunas
I woke up to the sound of a stone clattering to the floor.

My mother, Lamara, lives in a one-bedroom apartment within the gnome district of Mazrunas, close to the shore. Whenever I go to visit, she sets up a small cot for me, tucked between the kitchenette and the take. On that particular morning, Lamara was standing at the sink and struggling with a pestle and mortar.

As she picked up the pestle from the ground, she saw that I'd woken up and said, "I'm sorry, Hazel. I didn't mean to wake you."

"It's alright," I said as I got out of bed. "Here, let me do that."

She handed me the mortar, filled with psyllium husks and pellets of lime saltpetre and coal. I took over grinding them into a fine powder as she pulled a stool up to the fireplace to warm her hands. 

"When I visited in Nova, I left you with enough incense for six months. What happened to the rest?"

"Vernum was too wet this year. It all went bad." We sat in silence for a while as I worked. Once I finished, I poured the powder onto a plate for her 

"I can grind the rest now if you have more." Lamara gestured to a tin box in the corner of the room. I brought it over to the kitchenette, cracked the wax sealing the lid, and started moving the contents into the mortar. Lamara took the plate and poured a bit of water in, using her fingers to mix it until it has the consistency of clay. She moulded that into small cones, which she set aside to dry.

While we worked, Lamara asked me about the [[Arcaneum]]. I told her about my studies, the professors, friends, rivals. As I finish grinding the incense powder, I pulled a bag of coffee grounds from under the sink and set a kettle on. I asked her about her neighbors, her health, her whist group.

Once the coffee is done, Lamara gently gathered up a shawl and a dried incense cone. I poured out two cups and we headed down to the courtyard outside the building. She wrapped herself in the shawl, lit the incense, and whispered a prayer to the setting moon. Then we say together on the grass, idly chatting as we waited for the sunrise.
